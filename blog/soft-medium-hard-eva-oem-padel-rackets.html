Soft vs Medium vs Hard EVA for OEM Padel Rackets: A Buyer’s Guide

A practical guide for padel brands, distributors, wholesalers and product-development teams specifying EVA foam for private-label and custom racket programs.

EVA foam is an important part of padel racket construction, but no single EVA hardness suits every product program. For OEM buyers, the useful question is which foam direction fits the intended playing feel, rebound response, control, target player profile, racket shape, weight, balance and overall construction. A soft, medium or hard EVA choice should therefore be treated as a development decision, not as a standalone quality ranking.

Short answer: Soft EVA generally creates a softer, more cushioned response. Medium EVA provides a balanced compromise between comfort, rebound and feedback. Hard EVA generally produces a firmer, more direct response. Final performance depends on the complete racket construction, not on hardness alone.

What Is EVA Foam in a Padel Racket?

EVA, or ethylene-vinyl acetate, is a polymer foam used as the internal core material in many padel rackets. The core sits between the racket faces and works with the frame to manage impact deformation, rebound behavior, vibration and overall feel.

When comparing an EVA padel racket specification, OEM buyers should ask about more than a soft, medium or hard label. Relevant variables include foam formulation, density, hardness, cell structure, thickness, compression characteristics and rebound behavior. The relationship between the core, frame and face construction is equally important. A core with one commercial label can behave differently when paired with a different carbon fiber face, resin system, layup, frame structure or racket shape.

Factories may use different terminology for EVA grades, so a buyer should request a written specification and a representative sample. Unsupported laboratory specifications should not be inferred from a short material label.

Soft EVA Padel Rackets

A soft EVA direction typically gives a softer impact sensation, more cushioning and more comfortable feedback. It may deform more easily under impact than a firmer foam, which can change the way the racket feels during contact.

That does not mean soft EVA automatically creates more rebound or more power. Actual response depends on the complete racket construction, including the face material, face stiffness, laminate layup, frame, shape, weight, balance and surface construction.

For an OEM or private-label program, soft EVA can be considered when comfort and a softer feel are priorities. The development brief should still define the target customer, desired feel, racket shape, weight, balance, face material and frame construction. Sample evaluation is needed before a factory quotation becomes a production specification.

Medium EVA Padel Rackets

Medium EVA is often used as a balanced development direction. It can provide moderate firmness, balanced impact feedback and a middle-ground response between softer cushioning and firmer directness.

This direction can be useful for a broad product program when a brand wants to avoid an extremely soft or extremely firm feel. However, medium EVA is not objectively the best choice. Its result depends on the face, layup, frame, racket shape, weight, balance and surface finish.

For a distributor or private-label buyer, a medium EVA sample can be one useful reference point in a structured comparison. The correct process is to compare samples built to the same target specification, rather than comparing a core label in isolation.

Hard EVA Padel Rackets

Hard EVA typically provides a firmer impact response, more direct feedback and less cushioning sensation than softer foam. It can have different rebound characteristics, but the direction of that response should be confirmed through the complete racket sample.

Hard EVA does not automatically produce more power. Perceived power is affected by racket shape, weight, balance, face stiffness, carbon fiber type, laminate layup, resin system, frame structure, surface construction and core behavior. A firmer core in one construction can feel very different from a firmer core in another.

OEM buyers considering hard EVA should define the target player profile, intended feel, face material, frame structure, shape and finished specifications. The factory should document the selected foam direction and confirm it against an approved sample.

Does EVA Hardness Affect Power and Control?

Short answer: EVA hardness can influence impact sensation, deformation and rebound behavior, but it should not be treated as a single-variable explanation of power or control.

Other factors include racket shape, balance point, static weight, swing weight, face material, carbon fiber type, layup, resin, frame structure, core thickness and surface texture. These variables interact. A racket with the same EVA label may feel different after a change in face construction or balance.

For OEM development, EVA should be tested as part of the complete racket construction rather than selected independently. A buyer should compare samples using an agreed brief and record feel, response, weight, balance and construction changes before locking the golden sample.

What Does 15D EVA Mean?

“15D” may be used commercially to describe a foam density or specification, but terminology and test methods can vary between suppliers. It should not be presented as a universal hardness standard.

International buyers communicating with Chinese factories should confirm the density specification, hardness or compression specification, test method, tolerance and sample consistency. They should also ask whether “15D” refers to a density convention, a supplier grade, or another internal description. The label alone is not enough to compare two factories’ EVA materials.

How OEM Buyers Should Choose EVA

Use the following framework as a starting point, then validate the result with samples:

Choose a softer EVA direction when:

  • comfort is a priority;
  • a softer impact feel is desired;
  • the target product positioning emphasizes cushioning.

Consider medium EVA when:

  • a balanced response is desired;
  • the product should serve a broader player segment;
  • the brand wants a middle-ground feel.

Consider harder EVA when:

  • firmer feedback is preferred;
  • a direct impact response is desired;
  • the target product positioning calls for a firmer feel.

These are development directions, not universal rules. The choice should be made with the target player, shape, weight, balance, face, frame and market position in view.

EVA Should Be Developed Together With the Racket Construction

EVA works as part of a system: EVA + carbon fiber face + layup + frame + shape + weight + balance + surface + resin. A soft EVA core combined with a stiff carbon face can produce a very different result from a soft EVA core combined with a more flexible construction.

That example does not establish a final performance outcome without laboratory testing. It shows why an OEM buyer should define the complete product brief and test the intended construction. A sample should represent the proposed face, core, frame, shape, finish, weight and balance rather than only the requested EVA label.

FAQ

Is soft EVA or hard EVA better for a padel racket?

Short answer: Neither is universally preferable. Soft and hard EVA are different development directions, so the suitable choice depends on the target feel and complete racket construction.

What is the difference between soft, medium and hard EVA?

Short answer: Soft EVA typically gives a more cushioned sensation, medium EVA a balanced response, and hard EVA a firmer, more direct response. The finished result also depends on the face, frame and other specifications.

Does harder EVA make a padel racket more powerful?

Short answer: Not automatically. Perceived power is affected by shape, weight, balance, face stiffness, layup, resin, frame structure, surface construction and core behavior as well as EVA.

What does 15D EVA mean?

Short answer: 15D may be used commercially to describe a foam density or specification, but terminology and test methods can vary. Confirm density, hardness, test method, tolerance and sample consistency.

Which EVA should OEM padel racket buyers choose?

Short answer: Choose the direction that fits the target player profile, desired feel, racket shape, weight, balance and market position. Validate the complete construction with samples.

Can an OEM padel racket manufacturer customize EVA hardness?

Short answer: An OEM program can discuss EVA formulation, density and hardness direction with a factory. The exact scope should be confirmed through specifications and sample evaluation.

Should EVA be tested with different carbon fiber faces?

Short answer: Yes. EVA should be evaluated with the intended face material and layup because changing the face construction can change the response of the same core.

What EVA specifications should a buyer request from a factory?

Short answer: Request the EVA type or formulation, density, hardness or compression specification, test method, tolerance, core thickness and sample consistency requirements.
