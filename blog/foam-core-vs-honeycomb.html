Foam Core vs PP Honeycomb Pickleball Paddles: Which to Choose

The core is the largest component in a pickleball paddle and the one that most determines feel. Foam and PP honeycomb cores play differently, and choosing between them is a positioning decision as much as a technical one. This guide compares both constructions on the factors that matter to buyers.

Two paddles with the same face material and the same thickness can feel completely different if their cores differ. That is because the core does most of the work in deciding how a paddle responds on contact: how much vibration reaches the hand, how wide the effective sweet spot is, and how the paddle sounds when it hits the ball.

This guide explains how PP honeycomb and foam cores work, what each does well, and how to choose the right platform for your brand's target player — whether you are building a training line, a premium range or both.

How pickleball paddle cores work

A pickleball paddle core is the layer between the two face surfaces, and it performs three jobs:

  • Weight control. The core is the largest volume in the paddle, so its density largely sets the total weight.
  • Response. How the core compresses and rebounds on impact shapes ball speed, feel and sound.
  • Stability. The core structure keeps the face flat through off-centre hits, defining the sweet spot.

Two core families dominate the market: honeycomb cores — most commonly PP (polypropylene) — and foam cores. They achieve these three jobs in different ways, and those differences map directly to player experience.

What is a PP honeycomb core?

A PP honeycomb core is made of hexagonal polypropylene cells arranged in a continuous sheet, with the cells running perpendicular to the paddle face. The honeycomb geometry is efficient: it provides structure with very little material, which keeps the paddle light.

On the Carbonova PP honeycomb platform, the construction combines a raw carbon face with a PP honeycomb core in a wide body shape with a cushioned grip:

  • Stable frame response — the honeycomb structure keeps the paddle stable through fast rallies and off-centre hits.
  • Lightweight response — a light build for quick hand speed and less fatigue during longer sessions.
  • Consistent feel — uniform face response across the sweet area, swing after swing.

PP honeycomb is the volume workhorse of the industry: it is well understood, predictable in production and suited to training, club and competition programs.

What is a foam core?

A foam core replaces the open honeycomb cells with a continuous foam layer inside the paddle. Instead of empty cells, the interior is filled with a dampening material that changes how energy moves through the paddle.

On the Carbonova GEN5 platform, the foam core is paired with a T700 raw carbon surface and a thermoformed unibody construction:

  • Expanded sweet spot — the continuous foam spreads impact response across a wider area.
  • Premium vibration control — the foam absorbs high-frequency vibration on contact, giving a softer, more controlled feel.
  • Quieter impact — foam cores produce a deeper, more muted contact sound that many players associate with premium paddles.

Foam cores have moved from entry-level paddles to premium competition platforms as manufacturing processes have improved. The GEN5 platform is an example of a foam core built for tournament-grade programs.

Foam core vs PP honeycomb: the comparison

  • Sweet spot. Foam cores spread the stable response area wider, making the paddle more forgiving on off-centre hits. Honeycomb cores concentrate the response more toward the geometric centre.
  • Feel and vibration. Foam dampens vibration, so impact feels softer and quieter. Honeycomb transfers more of the contact energy, which some players read as "crisper" feedback.
  • Weight. Honeycomb cores are highly efficient per unit of volume, supporting lighter builds and faster hand speed. Foam cores tend to allow a denser, more controlled feel at a similar or slightly higher weight.
  • Power. With the same face material, honeycomb cores generally return a livelier, poppier response. Foam cores pair naturally with stiff premium faces such as T700 carbon to deliver power with control.
  • Durability. Both constructions hold up in daily play; thermoformed builds — which fuse the core, faces and edge into one structure — add perimeter strength regardless of core type.

These are engineering tendencies, not absolutes — the final character depends on face material, thickness, shape and construction. What matters for your line is which tendency fits your target player.

A useful mental model for your product team: honeycomb cores are the "responsive" platform and foam cores are the "controlled" platform. When a buyer asks for a paddle that "feels alive", that is honeycomb territory; when they ask for a paddle that "feels solid and forgiving", that is foam territory. Framing the two platforms this way also gives your sales material two clean narratives instead of two overlapping feature lists.

Which players fit which core

Player segments map to cores more predictably than to face materials:

  • Honeycomb suits: players who want a light, lively, responsive paddle — from beginners who need an easy swing to intermediate players building hand speed, and control players who prefer crisp feedback.
  • Foam suits: players who prioritise consistency, comfort and a premium "solid" feel — including recreational and senior players who want less vibration, and competition players who want a wider margin for error.

If your brand targets a specific segment — a "comfort" story for recreational players or a "premium feel" story for advanced players — the core choice should come first, and the face material second.

The segmentation also drives retail merchandising: a honeycomb line can carry a "lightweight and responsive" shelf label, while a foam line sells itself with "expanded sweet spot" and "vibration control". Two cores, two shelf stories, one range.

Choosing a platform for your brand

Most ranges do not need to choose — they run both platforms in different tiers:

  • PP honeycomb as the volume and training tier, priced accessibly and sold on lightness and stability.
  • GEN5 foam core as the premium feel tier, sold on the expanded sweet spot, vibration control and T700 carbon power.

This structure gives retailers two distinct upsell stories and gives your team a clean answer when a buyer asks "what's the difference between your paddles?" If your range can only carry one platform, choose by your target player: the broad, price-sensitive market starts with honeycomb; the premium, feel-led market starts with foam.

How thickness, shape and construction interact with the core

The core does not act alone — thickness, shape and construction modify its character:

  • Thickness. A 16 mm core is the common performance thickness across Carbonova's platforms, whether honeycomb or foam. Thicker cores tend to dampen more and add stability; the trade-off is weight and hand speed.
  • Shape. Wide body shapes add face area and forgiveness, pairing naturally with a stable honeycomb platform for training programs. Elongated shapes concentrate the hitting area and pair with foam cores and stiff faces for a competition feel.
  • Construction. Thermoforming fuses the core, faces and edge into one structure, improving perimeter durability regardless of core type. Foam edge reinforcement extends the stable response area toward the rim on either platform.

When a factory quotes a "16 mm paddle", ask which core, shape and construction come with it — the same thickness number can produce very different paddles across these combinations.

Common misconceptions about cores

Two myths cause most buyer confusion, and both are worth clearing early:

  • "Foam cores are only for beginners." This is outdated. GEN5-style foam platforms are built for competition programs, pairing premium faces and thermoformed construction with the foam's feel advantages.
  • "Honeycomb is a low-quality material." PP honeycomb is a mature, well-engineered core used across training and competition paddles — its role is lightweight stability, and it does that job consistently.

The honest framing is positioning: honeycomb is the proven, price-efficient volume story; foam is the premium feel story. Neither is a quality downgrade, and a factory that runs both can help you place each platform at the right tier of your range.

Choose by player, not by trend

Foam cores are the premium story right now, and honeycomb cores are the proven volume story — both will be in the market for a long time. The right choice for your brand is the one that matches the player you are selling to and the price point you are targeting.
