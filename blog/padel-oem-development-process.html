Padel Racket OEM Development Process: From Mold Review to Release

A buyer-focused guide to the decisions, samples and production controls that turn a Padel racket concept into an approved OEM specification.

A Padel racket OEM program is easier to control when every stage has a defined input, a review point and an output that the next stage can use. Instead of treating development as a generic five-step workflow, buyers should document the racket brief, review the mold design, confirm samples, freeze the specification and define the quality release requirements.

Planning principle: MOQ is determined by the design and racket shape. Confirm the requested shape, materials, EVA direction, quantity and packaging scope before discussing the production plan.

What the OEM brief should define first

Start with the product direction and the information needed to make a sample. The available face-material choices include Fiberglass, UD, 3K, 12K, 18K and 24K. EVA options range from 10D to 25D. The brief should also state the racket shape, target weight, balance direction, surface finish, graphics, grip, packaging and intended market position.

Buyers can use the existing custom Padel racket manufacturing route to organize these decisions, then send the final project details through the RFQ form.

Stage 1: 3D mold drawing review

Input

Provide the intended racket shape, dimensions, drilling or hole pattern, frame direction, handle requirements, graphics references and any existing reference racket information.

Review

The Carbonova team reviews the 3D mold drawing before mold work starts. This is the point to check shape, proportions, handle interface, surface areas and the details that affect the requested product specification.

Output

An approved 3D mold direction that can be sent to the supporting mold-processing factory for mold manufacture. Open design questions should be recorded before the next stage.

Stage 2: Mold processing and trial mold

Input

The reviewed 3D mold drawing and the agreed design requirements.

Process

The supporting mold-processing factory manufactures the mold, followed by a trial mold check. The trial is used to verify that the mold direction can support the intended racket shape and the production process under the project team's control.

Output

A trial-mold result and a decision on whether the project can move into material preparation and forming. Any required mold adjustment should be documented before cutting begins.

Stage 3: Cutting, preforming and hot pressing

Input

Approved mold direction, selected face materials, EVA direction from 10D to 25D, frame and core requirements, and the agreed construction brief.

Process

The controlled process moves through cutting, preforming and hot-press forming. These stages are controlled by the project team and create the formed racket structure according to the approved mold and material direction.

Output

A formed sample for inspection and review. The sample should be checked against the requested shape, construction, weight direction and balance direction before finishing work is treated as final.

Stage 4: Fine finishing, labeling and assembly

Input

The formed racket sample, approved artwork, logo placement, surface-finish direction, grip requirements and packaging requirements.

Process

The process continues through fine coating, then labeling or UV printing, followed by assembly and packaging. The branding method should be selected in the project specification so the sample and production reference use the same direction.

Output

A finished sample that represents the proposed appearance, labeling, assembly and packaging approach. Record any difference between the artwork reference and the finished sample before approval.

Stage 5: Sample confirmation and specification freeze

Sample confirmation is the decision gate between development and repeatable production. Compare the finished sample with the brief and record the approved shape, face material, EVA direction, weight, balance, surface finish, graphics, grip and packaging.

After the sample is accepted, freeze the production specification. A frozen specification gives the factory a clear reference for the next production review and prevents later changes from being treated as informal instructions. If the buyer changes a material, shape, artwork or balance direction after the freeze, the change should be recorded and reviewed as a new specification decision.

Stage 6: Capacity planning and production allocation

The workshop has 10 Padel hot-press machines, and each machine can be equipped with 5 sets of molds. Each machine has a monthly capacity of 2,250 rackets, giving a total of 22,500 rackets per month across 10 machines when using the stated full-configuration, project-allocation and capacity basis.

This capacity figure is not a promise for every project. Actual allocation depends on the design, racket shape, mold setup, project assignment and the approved production plan. Confirm the project quantity and mold requirements when submitting the RFQ.

For broader factory context, review the factory capability page and the Padel platform overview.

Stage 7: QC and release

Input

The frozen specification, approved sample, artwork and packaging reference.

QC focus

Quality control should compare the production output with the approved reference across the documented shape, materials, EVA direction, finish, labeling, assembly and packaging requirements. The release decision should be tied to the agreed specification rather than to an informal verbal change.

Output

A documented release decision for the project lot, with any non-conformance or correction recorded before packing and shipment coordination. Buyers can also review the existing quality control page when preparing their supplier questions.

RFQ checklist for a Padel OEM project

  1. Racket shape and reference images or sample
  2. Target market and player profile
  3. Face material direction: Fiberglass, UD, 3K, 12K, 18K or 24K
  4. EVA direction between 10D and 25D
  5. Target weight and balance direction
  6. Surface finish, drilling or hole-pattern requirements
  7. Logo, graphics, label or UV-printing requirements
  8. Grip, assembly and packaging requirements
  9. Estimated quantity and project allocation needs
  10. Sample confirmation and specification-freeze expectations
  11. QC and release requirements

A complete RFQ helps the factory evaluate the design and racket shape that determine MOQ, then align the mold, material, sample and production discussion around the same specification.
